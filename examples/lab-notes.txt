The lab — what the specimen processes are
====================================================================

Six small Python programs, each with its
behaviour stated in advance, so that every
tool in this guide can be checked against a
known answer. None of them does anything
but demonstrate; all of them stop on their
own after the seconds you give them (600 by
default).

lab-spinner.py [seconds]
    Burns one CPU core in a busy loop.
    Watching tools should show it in state
    R, at or near the top of any CPU sort,
    with TIME climbing steadily. On an
    unmetered machine its %CPU sits near
    100 (one core); on a shared or metered
    one it will read lower and share the
    core — the guide's build machine
    showed exactly that.

lab-memory-eater.py [megabytes] [seconds]
    Allocates the stated megabytes (200 in
    the guide), touches one byte per page
    so the memory is resident, and holds
    it while sleeping. HARD-CAPPED at
    300 MB inside the script: the lab must
    never be able to summon the OOM killer
    on its own machine. Asking for more
    gets you 300 and a note.

lab-sleeper.py [seconds]
    Sleeps. State S, no CPU, tiny memory:
    "running but idle," for contrast with
    the spinner in every table.

lab-churner.py [seconds]
    Starts a `sleep 1` child, waits for
    it, pauses, repeats. There is usually
    one very young grandchild alive under
    it — and sometimes, between children,
    none. The specimen for processes too
    short-lived to catch by looking twice.

lab-family.py [seconds]
    A parent that starts lab-sleeper and
    lab-churner as its children and waits
    for them: one household, three
    generations when the churner's child
    is alive, for pstree and ps --forest.

lab-zombie-parent.py [seconds]
    Forks a child that exits immediately,
    then sleeps WITHOUT reaping it. The
    child stays in the table as a zombie
    (state Z, <defunct>) for the duration.
    The parent reaps properly on its way
    out, so nothing outlives the specimen.

lab-stubborn.py [seconds]
    Ignores SIGTERM and keeps sleeping.
    TERM fails on it (that is the lesson);
    KILL still works, and so does waiting
    for its seconds to run out.

Starting and stopping
    sh start-lab.sh [seconds]   starts the
        spinner, the eater (200 MB), the
        family, and the zombie parent, and
        records each PID with its start
        time and name in .lab-pids.
    sh stop-lab.sh               checks
        each recorded process is still
        the one that was started (a bare
        PID is not proof -- PIDs get
        recycled), TERMs those PIDs and
        their children, then KILLs any
        survivors, and removes .lab-pids.
    Specimens also retire on their own
    when their seconds expire, started
    either way.

Safety, stated once
    The eater is capped. The spinner is
    one core, not all of them. The zombie
    holds nothing but a table entry. The
    stubborn one can be KILLed at any
    time. Nothing in this lab writes to
    disk, opens a socket, or survives its
    own timer — the worst it can do is
    make one core warm for ten minutes.
